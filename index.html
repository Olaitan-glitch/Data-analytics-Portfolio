Patient Satisfaction and Service Quality Analysis for Bupa Healthcare Service Using Excel and Power Bi
nulla imperdiet

Bupa is a leading international healthcare organization that provides health insurance, healthcare services, and patient-centered care. The organization focuses on improving patient experiences, delivering quality healthcare services, and ensuring operational efficiency. Patient satisfaction is an important measure of healthcare performance because it reflects patients’ experiences with medical services, staff communication, appointment accessibility, waiting times, and complaint management. This project involved analyzing a simulated Bupa Healthcare Patient Satisfaction and Service Quality dataset. The dataset was created for educational and analytical purposes and does not represent actual Bupa patient records or verified organizational performance. The purpose of the analysis was to evaluate patient satisfaction, assess service performance, identify potential areas of concern, and provide data-driven recommendations to support healthcare management decisions. The analysis focused on common challenges faced by BUPA healthcare , including long patient waiting times, difficulties accessing appointments, inconsistent communication, patient complaints, and low survey participation. These challenges can negatively affect patient experiences and service quality. The project aimed to investigate these issues, identify patterns across departments and service categories, and determine where management should prioritize improvement efforts. Microsoft Excel, Power Query, Power BI, and DAX were the primary tools used for the project. Excel was used for initial data exploration and preparation, while Power Query supported data cleaning and transformation. Power BI was used for data modeling, KPI development, interactive dashboard creation, and visualization. DAX formulas were developed to calculate performance indicators and support meaningful comparisons across different service categories. The data cleaning process involved checking for duplicate records and identifiers, missing values, inconsistent category labels, incorrect data types, invalid satisfaction ratings, and inconsistent date formats. Data validation included reviewing unique identifiers, verifying relationships between tables, checking the consistency of survey and complaint records, and ensuring that calculations used appropriate numerators and denominators. These checks were important for improving data reliability and preventing misleading analytical results. Following data preparation, key performance indicators were developed to measure patient satisfaction and healthcare service quality. These included average satisfaction score, positive and negative satisfaction rates, neutral satisfaction rate, survey response rate, average waiting time, communication rating, appointment accessibility, discharge experience, complaint volume, complaint rate, and complaint resolution rate. Each KPI was defined using clear calculation logic to ensure accurate interpretation and consistent reporting. Three interactive Power BI dashboards were developed to communicate the findings. The Patient Satisfaction Dashboard provided an overview of satisfaction levels, rating distributions, and patient feedback. The Service Performance Dashboard examined waiting times, communication, appointment accessibility, discharge experiences, and departmental performance. The Complaints and Survey Participation Dashboard focused on complaint patterns, resolution performance, and patient survey engagement. Interactive slicers and visualizations enabled management to explore performance across departments, appointment types, service channels, and reporting periods. Data storytelling was used to transform the dashboard results into meaningful insights and management recommendations. The simulated results showed an average satisfaction score of 4.02 out of 5, with 73.9% positive satisfaction, 21.2% neutral satisfaction, and 4.9% negative satisfaction. These findings suggest generally positive patient experiences among survey respondents, while the relatively high neutral satisfaction rate indicates opportunities for further improvement. Out of 1,500 eligible surveys, 975 were completed, representing a 65% survey completion rate. This highlights the importance of strengthening patient engagement and improving survey participation to obtain more representative feedback. The analysis also highlighted the need to investigate waiting times, appointment accessibility, communication quality, and complaint resolution processes. These factors were identified as important areas for further investigation rather than confirmed causes of dissatisfaction. Additional correlation and statistical analysis would help establish whether they are significantly associated with patient satisfaction. Based on the findings, recommendations included improving appointment scheduling processes, monitoring and reducing excessive waiting times, strengthening staff communication, reviewing recurring complaint categories, improving complaint resolution efficiency, and encouraging greater patient participation in satisfaction surveys. Management was also encouraged to monitor KPIs regularly and use the interactive dashboards to identify emerging service-quality concerns and evaluate improvement initiatives.
